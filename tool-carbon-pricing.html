What it is
Carbon and Energy Pricing Tool
hero screenshot

Carbon pricing mechanisms are key climate policy tools that push companies towards a low-carbon future. They're designed to make sure businesses pay for the social and environmental costs of their carbon emissions — by raising the cost of carbon-intensive activities, carbon taxes and carbon markets push companies to decarbonise and adopt more climate-friendly processes.

There are two main types. Regulatory carbon pricing is where governments require firms in certain industries to pay for their carbon emissions, usually via a carbon tax or a cap-and-trade system. Internal carbon pricing is where a company voluntarily applies a price to carbon emissions within its own operations and/or supply chain, to drive its own decarbonisation efforts. The tool calculates a company's exposure to both.

What the tool does

Anticipate future costs

Forecasts additional financial costs tied to carbon and energy prices — across natural gas, coal, gasoline, fuel and electricity in transport, industry, building and power production — through scenario-based impact studies.

Facilitate transformation strategies

Supports the development of business model transformation strategies, from decarbonising core activities to transitioning to greener supply chains.

Evaluate financial impact

Evaluates the financial impact of transformation and decarbonisation actions, with annual updates to a company's CO2 emissions data and regular refreshes of energy and carbon price information.

In-depth modelling

Country and sector-level scenarios to 2050

Tool interface
screenshot

The tool models, at a country and sector level, three scenarios of energy and carbon price development up to 2050 — covering the cost of energy procurement and carbon-regulation compliance across the value chain, the financial impact of the most significant sources of CO2 emissions, and the savings achieved through emissions-mitigation plans. Modular charts and graphs give a fast, visual read on the key messages, making it easy to spot the most exposed service lines and products that need immediate action.

Additional tool features

Identify your risk

Surfaces the existing and upcoming regulatory mechanisms that carry financial implications for a business and its suppliers.

Quantify your exposure

Calculates the degree to which carbon emissions will increase operational and supply chain costs in the years ahead.

Establish your carbon price

Engages senior stakeholders and helps design a tailor-made internal carbon price fit to drive real transformation.

Monitor the results

Tracks the success of a carbon pricing strategy over time, and what to prioritise next.

Key questions on carbon pricing

What is carbon pricing?

Carbon pricing is a mechanism that captures the external costs of greenhouse gas emissions — the costs the public otherwise bears, such as damage to crops or property loss from extreme weather. Carbon pricing links these costs back to their source through a price, typically a cost associated with each tonne of emitted CO2.

Why is carbon pricing an effective climate action tool?

Carbon pricing shifts the responsibility for the damage caused by emissions onto the entities responsible for them, while leaving them the choice of how to respond — modify their practices and reduce emissions, or continue emitting while paying for it. That flexibility lets the same environmental goal be reached in the most cost-effective way for society as a whole, while internalising the external cost of climate change into everyday economic decisions and creating an incentive for faster decarbonisation and investment.

What are the two main types of carbon pricing mechanism?

Regulatory carbon pricing: governments require firms in certain industries to pay for their carbon emissions, usually via a carbon tax or cap-and-trade system. Internal carbon pricing: companies voluntarily apply a price to carbon emissions within their own operations and/or supply chain to drive their decarbonisation efforts.
